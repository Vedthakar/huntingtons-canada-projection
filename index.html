How the model runs

Carriers are tracked in single-year age cohorts from 2026 to 2076. Each year every cohort ages, dies at a background rate plus an excess hazard once symptomatic, and produces children at Canadian age-specific fertility. Each child of a carrier inherits the expansion with probability 0.5 — the autosomal dominant rule — and that stream is what the policy multiplies by 1 − coverage × efficacy.

Onset is a logistic curve with median age 45, so a carrier is counted as a diagnosed case only once onset has occurred. The baseline is calibrated so prevalence per capita holds flat without intervention: absolute case counts then rise purely because the population rises, which is the comparison the case asks for.

Why no ancestry term

Prevalence does differ between populations — about 10–14 per 100,000 in European-ancestry populations against under 1 per 100,000 in East Asian populations. That gap is a founder effect: differing background frequencies of the haplotypes that predispose to CAG expansion, laid down historically.

It is not a difference in how the allele is transmitted. Every carrier passes the expansion to half their conceptions regardless of ancestry. Building the projection on shifting racial composition would model the wrong mechanism and hand the opposing side an easy attack. The single national prevalence rate, adjustable above, carries the same information without that exposure.

What the coverage slider really means

Your proposed amendment is narrow: autosomal dominant disease where one parent is homozygous, so that no unaffected embryo exists and PGT-M has nothing to select. Homozygosity is roughly 0.3% of Huntington's cases. Set honestly, that is coverage near 1% — the Narrow exception preset — and the two lines are visually indistinguishable for the whole window.

That is not a defect in the argument. It is the argument: the amendment is a targeted response to an absolute medical necessity, not a public health program. The higher presets show what broader eligibility would buy, which is the question the panel will ask next.

The floor the model will not cross

Even at 100% coverage and 100% efficacy, the orange line flattens rather than falling to zero. New expansions keep arising from intermediate alleles carried by parents with no family history and no reason to be screened.

Huntington's is reducible, not eliminable. Any claim that germline editing "ends" the disease is false, and the de novo slider is there so you can show exactly where the floor sits under your own assumptions.
